
### **Introduction to plural nouns**

When we make a noun plural in English, we almost always just add an "s" at the end of the word.


<table>
  <tr>
   <td>ball → ball<span style="text-decoration:underline;">s</span>
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td>number → number<span style="text-decoration:underline;">s</span>
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td>tree → tree<span style="text-decoration:underline;">s</span>
   </td>
  </tr>
</table>


Many plural nouns in English end in "-s", but the sound we make is not usually *s*. Most of these words end with a *z* sound.


<table>
  <tr>
   <td><strong>balls</strong> sounds like <em>ballz</em>
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td><strong>numbers</strong> sounds like <em>numberz</em>
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td><strong>trees</strong> sounds like <em>treez</em>
   </td>
  </tr>
</table>


Did you know the plural for "sandwich" is "sandwich**es**"? 😯 \
That's because this word ends with "**ch**" so we add an "**e**" before the "**s**" to make it easier to pronounce! \
 \
So, if the singular word ends in "**ch**", or even "**s**", "**sh**" or "**x**", then add "<span style="text-decoration:underline;">es</span>" to make it plural!


<table>
  <tr>
   <td>sandwi<strong>ch</strong> → sandwi<strong>ch</strong><span style="text-decoration:underline;">es</span>
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td>bu<strong>s</strong> → bu<strong>s</strong><span style="text-decoration:underline;">es</span>
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td>di<strong>sh</strong> → di<strong>sh</strong><span style="text-decoration:underline;">es</span>
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td>bo<strong>x</strong> → bo<strong>x</strong><span style="text-decoration:underline;">es</span>
   </td>
  </tr>
</table>


**Exceptions**

There are a few more exceptions to these rules that you will learn as you improve. \
 \
There are also some words that have completely irregular plural forms. The five most useful ones to learn are these:


<table>
  <tr>
   <td>child → children
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td>woman → women
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td>man → men
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td>person → people
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td>foot → feet
   </td>
  </tr>
</table>


**Be careful!**

There are some "-o" and "-y" words that don't follow the rules.


<table>
  <tr>
   <td><strong>If the "-y" word has a vowel (a, e, i, o, u) right before the "y", then the word behaves like regular nouns (just add "s").</strong>
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td>boy → boy<span style="text-decoration:underline;">s</span>
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td>key → key<span style="text-decoration:underline;">s</span>
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td>day → day<span style="text-decoration:underline;">s</span>
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td><strong>Some "-o" words (especially words borrowed from Italian) do not add the "e" and behave like regular nouns.</strong>
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td>piano → piano<span style="text-decoration:underline;">s</span>
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td>studio → studio<span style="text-decoration:underline;">s</span>
   </td>
  </tr>
  <tr>
   <td>casino → casino<span style="text-decoration:underline;">s</span>
   </td>
  </tr>
</table>
